\subsection{Software baselines: accuracy}\label{sec:eval-pl}
Figure~\ref{fig:pl} shows P\textsubscript{L} per $d$-round block for PyMatching and both QSX-UF variants over
$p\in[0.001,0.01]$ and $d\in\{3,5,7,9\}$. Each point runs until a target number of failures or a shot cap is reached,
and carries an exact Clopper--Pearson 95\% interval \stage{Monte Carlo, Stim}. At $p=0.003$, weighted QSX-UF
has P\textsubscript{L} $=\claim{M1_PL_d7_p003_UF}$ against $\claim{M1_PL_d7_p003_PM}$ for PyMatching at $d=7$

\subsection{Hardware: cycles, resources and latency}\label{sec:eval-hw}
\paragraph{Where the cycles go.} The cycle-exact model splits every decode into label propagation, the
LANES-wide scans (cluster parity/boundary and the activity broadcast), FSM control, BFS distances and peeling. An
identity on every shot checks the split exactly (Fig.~\ref{fig:cyc}). At LANES$=8$ the scans take
\claim{M2_scan_share_d5_L8}\% of all cycles at $d=5$ and \claim{M2_scan_share_d7_L8}\% at $d=7$. With one lane per
vertex (LANES$=V$) they drop to \claim{M2_scan_share_d7_LV}\% at $d=7$, and the mean cost falls from
\claim{M2_mean_cyc_d7_L8} to \claim{M2_mean_cyc_d7_LV} cycles. The compute-unit parameter is therefore the main
latency lever of this microarchitecture \stage{sim}.

(\texttt{results/M2/fpga/critical\_paths.json}).

\paragraph{Latency vs.\ the CPU.} Figure~\ref{fig:hwcpu} compares the best placed configuration per distance with
single-thread CPU decoding of the same shots. At $d=3$ the RTL p99 is \claim{M2_hw_p99_ns_d3}\,ns
(\claim{M2_hw_best_d3}), against \claim{M2_cpu_pm_p99_us_d3}\,\textmu s for PyMatching, a ratio of
\claim{M2_ratio_p99_d3}. At $d=5$ it is \claim{M2_hw_p99_ns_d5}\,ns against \claim{M2_cpu_pm_p99_us_d5}\,\textmu s
(ratio \claim{M2_ratio_p99_d5}). On this device the hardware decoder is \emph{not} faster in typical or p99
latency. Its advantage lies in the tail. The worst case observed in hardware is \claim{M2_hw_max_ns_d3}\,ns at
$d=3$, against \claim{M2_cpu_pm_max_us_d3}\,\textmu s for the CPU, which is \claim{M2_ratio_max_d3}$\times$ lower.
More importantly, the hardware bound is guaranteed rather than observed. Property C2 proves that a decode never
exceeds its \texttt{cycle\_budget}, and the shell proofs guarantee that a frame over budget comes back as a
\texttt{WATCHDOG} status, never as a wrong valid result. Against a block deadline of $d\times 1$\,\textmu s
(an assumed round time), with the budget set to fit, the hardware finishes all but \claim{M2_hw_fallback_d3}\% of
$d=3$ frames itself. At $d=5$ it finishes only \claim{M2_hw_within_d5}\% of them; the rest go to the
verified software path.

\subsection{Cost of the verified mechanisms}
Table~\ref{tab:overhead} separates the decoder core from the verified shell with a hierarchical synthesis
\stage{synth}. The shell contains the commit-on-last frame FIFO (backpressure, I1/P3), the atomic configuration
session with its written-word bitmap and range check (P5/P6), and the dispatcher, round-tag path and status gating
(P1--P4). At $d=5$ it costs \claim{M3_shell_lut_d5} LUT4 against \claim{M3_core_lut_d5} for the core
(\claim{M3_shell_lut_pct_d5}\%), but \claim{M3_shell_ff_pct_d5}\% of the flip-flops. Most of those FFs hold the
threshold table, which any configurable decoder needs; the atomicity bookkeeping itself is one bit per configuration
word plus one range-error bit. In time, framing and the handshakes add \claim{M3_shell_cycles_d3},
\claim{M3_shell_cycles_d5} and \claim{M3_shell_cycles_d7} cycles per frame at $d=3,5,7$ \stage{sim}: the input
header and syndrome beats, the dispatcher's load and the output header. Formal-only logic sits under
\texttt{ifdef FORMAL} and is not synthesised.

\subsection{Extensions}\label{sec:ext}
\paragraph{Learned decoding.} A small message-passing GNN over the decoding graph, trained on Stim data at
several $p$, reaches P\textsubscript{L} $=\claim{M6_pl_d5_GNN}$ at $d=5$, $p=0.003$, against \claim{M6_pl_d5_UF}
for QSX-UF and \claim{M6_pl_d5_PM} for PyMatching, i.e.\ \claim{M6_gnn_ratio_d5}$\times$ PyMatching. At $d=3$ the
ratio is \claim{M6_gnn_ratio_d3}. Its single-thread CPU inference takes \claim{M6_gnn_b1_p50_us_d5}\,\textmu s at
batch 1 and \claim{M6_gnn_batched_us_d5}\,\textmu s per shot batched \stage{CPU}. That is far slower than either
classical decoder. It is a baseline, not a competitor to large learned decoders \cite{bausch2024alphaqubit}.

\paragraph{Adaptive policy under noise shifts.} An online policy estimates $(p,\,m)$ from two window statistics
(the detection-event density and the fraction of adjacent defect pairs joined by time-like edges). It then
reconfigures the accelerator's threshold table through the verified atomic session (P5/P6), or routes windows to
PyMatching (``hybrid''). We compare it with fixed decoders and with oracles that know the true noise
(Fig.~\ref{fig:adapt}). After a step change in $p$, the hybrid policy matches the PyMatching oracle
(\claim{M6_step_adaptivehybrid} vs.\ \claim{M6_step_oraclePM}) within \claim{M6_recovery_shots} shots. Re-weighting
QSX-UF alone did \emph{not} help: adaptive and oracle UF are at \claim{M6_step_adaptiveUF} and
\claim{M6_step_oracleUF}, against \claim{M6_step_fixedUF} for fixed weights. The median-normalised 3-bit thresholds are
nearly invariant under a uniform change of $p$. The policy costs at most \claim{M6_policy_ns}\,ns per shot
\stage{CPU}; its statistics are counters that the shell could compute in hardware.

\paragraph{Federated training with differential privacy.} Three simulated sites have different noise
(Table~\ref{tab:m7}). FedAvg with pairwise-mask secure aggregation approaches centralized training. The first DP-SGD
configuration (lot 512) gave P\textsubscript{L} that did not depend on $\sigma$. Ablations traced this to a plateau
that DP-limited training converges to, rather than to clipping (DECISIONS D9/D10, \texttt{results/M7/dp\_debug.json}).
With large lots (8192), privacy from $\varepsilon=\claim{M7_max_eps}$ down to $\varepsilon=\claim{M7_knee_eps}$
($\delta=10^{-5}$, RDP accountant) costs nothing beyond that plateau. At $\varepsilon=\claim{M7_below_knee_eps}$
P\textsubscript{L} rises at least \claim{M7_below_knee_factor}$\times$ at every site. Tripling the step budget lets
the noisiest run reach the plateau, but only at a higher $\varepsilon$. This is a single-process simulation; the
masks assume honest-but-curious parties and no dropouts.

\paragraph{Secure experiment infrastructure.} Syndrome traces and result manifests are Ed25519-signed, results are
stored with AES-256-GCM, and runs are started and stopped through an mTLS control API, all with the
\texttt{cryptography} library and Python's \texttt{ssl}. \claim{M8_tests} tests pass (\claim{M8_tests_failed}
failures), including the negative cases: a tampered trace, a manifest with a wrong key, an unsigned or replayed
command, and a missing or untrusted client certificate are all rejected. Verification runs at
\claim{M8_verify_mbps}\,MB/s and encryption at \claim{M8_aes_enc_mbps}\,MB/s. A fresh mTLS connection costs
\claim{M8_mtls_p50_ms}\,ms (p50), against \claim{M8_plain_p50_ms}\,ms for plain HTTP \stage{CPU}.

\paragraph{End to end.} The integration demo (\texttt{bench/e2e\_demo.py}) samples \claim{E2E_shots} Stim shots and
streams them through the RTL accelerator in Verilator. The accelerator is configured by an atomic session with a
small growth-round limit, and \claim{E2E_mm} frames mismatch the model. The host implements
\texttt{QSXProtocol.tla} and asserts its safety invariants at run time. \claim{E2E_hw} rounds were committed from
hardware and \claim{E2E_sw} from the software fallback, covering \claim{E2E_timeouts} hardware timeouts and
\claim{E2E_lost} injected lost responses. The committed predictions are signed and stored encrypted. A browser view
(M9, \texttt{viz/}) verifies the signature with WebCrypto and replays the trace through the Wasm decoder,
reproducing the committed predictions (\claim{E2E_replay}).
